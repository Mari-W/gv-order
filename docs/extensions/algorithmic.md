# A. Algorithmic typing

Goal. Type the examples without hand-written derivations, worlds and rank
proofs. Completeness is not the goal. The declarative system of spec §9 stays the
reference, and the algorithm is sound for it.

## Outline

1. Linear structure. Session types, environment splitting with input and output
   contexts, and conversion on regular session types. Standard GV techniques make
   this decidable. Points play no role here.
2. Fact generation. A judgment Θ; Δ ⊢ t : T ⇝ 𝒞 generates fresh point variables
   at new, at λ (μ, ℓ) and at force. It solves ≡ by union-find with congruence
   closure for successor and carried roots, which also fixes the payload points
   of send and recv. It collects the ≺ atoms in 𝒞.
   Rocq: every strict world that satisfies 𝒞 under an assignment yields a typed
   derivation at that world.
3. Non-recursive fragment. Without boxes 𝒞 is finite. The world is its closure
   and strictness is a cycle check modulo ≡, in polynomial time. This types sync,
   cycle, scheduler and delegation, and rejects blocked and bad link.
4. Boxes with annotations. The programmer writes ∀ιs and C. Two fragments are
   compared by which examples they type.
   - Decidable. Finite C plus counter facts with constant offsets, each counter
     once per side. A recursive call is matching under a counter shift.
     The section on decidability below analyses strictness for this fragment.
   - SMT. Linear arithmetic over offsets. Strictness by a ranking certificate
     (linear or lexicographic) that the solver finds and Rocq checks.
5. Parser and checker. A small front end for writing example programs and running
   them through the algorithm.
6. Recursion and inference of C. To be discussed. One heuristic closes the facts
   of one round under the call substitution, C = ⋃ₖ C₀[σᵏ(ιs)].

## Open questions

- Which fragment types the four recursive examples (request and reply, relay,
  tail call, received chain) and the forwarder.
- Whether certificates for strictness should be checked in Rocq or trusted.

## Decidability of the counter fragment

State of 2026-10-30, aligned with ORDERS-SPEC.md after sharp edges 67.
Paper-level. Nothing here is mechanized. Notation follows ORDERS-SPEC.md §4, §5
and §9. A carried root is written x.i with positions from 1. So (c,k).1 is the
first channel carried by c's message at its action k, and (c,k).1 + b is that
channel after b of its own actions.

### 0  Scope and results

The static system of ORDERS-SPEC §9 needs no decision procedure. Its box
constraints are arbitrary sets of positive facts, and its side conditions are
entailments (sharp edges 65). This section studies decidability for a
candidate fragment of constraints, for a later algorithmic system. The fragment
has counter facts ∀κ̄. x ≺ y whose coordinates are constants or a counter plus
an offset, with each counter at most once per side. Section 1 defines it as the
schema class L.

An algorithmic checker meets three questions. The world W_G generated by a
finite set G of facts and aliases must be strict. Side conditions inside box
bodies must follow from W_G and the hypotheses in scope. And each force at
ground points must find its constraint in W_G. The third question puts counter
schemas into G, because the constraint of a recursive box is an invariant that
states every round at once. A recursion that moves its points by σ in each
round, with the facts C₀ of one round, has the invariant
C = ⋃ₖ C₀[σᵏ(ιs)/ιs]. A force at ground xs asks every fact of C[xs/ιs].

The results are these.

- Strictness of W_G is decidable for every finite G in L that satisfies five
  syntactic side conditions and a sixth that holds for every example. The five
  are diagonal freedom (D), role consistency (R), offset alignment (A), equal
  coefficients (C) and monotone boundary roles (M). The sixth is finite shape
  closure (S), which is not shown decidable here (Theorems 1 to 4). When no role
  carries a constant the procedure is polynomial in the size of the static graph
  𝒢 of §2. Otherwise it is polynomial in the size of an abstract graph that
  grows exponentially in the number of constant-carrying roles per shape.
- Without (M) strictness is undecidable, already for unit schemas without
  aliases and with nesting depth 3 (Theorem 5, a reduction from two-counter
  machines). Boundary facts therefore do not reduce by unrolling in general.
- A side condition in a box body under finitely many ≺ hypotheses reduces to at
  most (2|Ψ| + 2)² ground queries and a search in a graph on 2|Ψ| + 2 vertices
  (Lemma 5). A ground query reduces to strictness of G plus one fact (Lemma 6).
  The choice of G reduces to relations on the finitely many ground points that
  the open conditions mention (Lemma 7).
- Ground G lies in L without counters. It satisfies (D), (C) and (A) outright,
  and (R) and (S) when its aliases relate shapes of equal depth. Delegation
  emits aliases of unequal depth, and a channel that returns over its own
  carrier violates (M) (§8.2). Strictness for those ground G is open.
- The force obligation reduces to strictness of G together with one schema
  family per fact of C (Lemma 8). For ≺ constraints these families contain no
  alias schemas (Lemma 9).
- Invariants whose rounds move by pure shifts with diagonal or axis generators
  fall under Theorems 2 to 4. A single map that moves into carried roots along
  dependency cycles without carried entries gives one counter per side. The
  relay lands there and violates (M), which is the open case of §10.1. Several
  maps that do not commute have a decidable depth test. Unbounded depth, sum
  coordinates and scalings are open (§9).
- The fragment cannot express an invariant that reaches carried roots of every
  depth. The received chain of ORDERS-SPEC §13 has such an invariant (§1.4).

Offset alignment (A) answers gap G2 of docs/history/DECIDABILITY-REVIEW.md. It
rejects an alias that carries a counter at the offset on one side and at an
index coordinate on the other, such as the delegation alias (e, κ) ≡ (c, κ).1
in every round of a recursion. A forced ≺ invariant produces no such alias
(Lemma 9).

Proved here are Theorems 1 to 5 and Lemmas 1 to 9. Cited are the
linear-programming characterization of zero multicycles and the recursive SCC
scheme of Kosaraju and Sullivan (STOC 1988, pp. 399–401), polynomial-time
linear programming, and the undecidability of halting for two-counter machines
(Minsky 1961). We re-prove the parts of Kosaraju–Sullivan we change.

### 1  The schema class

ORDERS-SPEC §16 names the fragment only as "facts ∀κ̄. x ≺ y whose coordinates
are constants or κ + n, with each counter at most once per side". This section
fixes it as a class of schemas. Every result below is relative to this choice.

#### 1.1  Families

A shape is a point constant together with a finite sequence of positions,
f = a.i₁…iₙ. The depth of f is n. A ground point of shape f arises from (a, b₁)
by the operations .i₁, + b₂, .i₂, + b₃, …, .iₙ, + b_{n+1}, applied from left to
right.

```
(a, b₁).i₁ + b₂ ⋯ .iₙ + b_{n+1}        with coordinates  (b₁, …, b_{n+1}) ∈ ℕ^{n+1}
```

So the vertices of shape f form a copy of ℕ^{d_f} with d_f = n+1. The last
coordinate is the offset. Program order moves only the offset. A carried root
moves the offset into a new index coordinate and starts a new offset at 0.

```
(p, b) + 1  = (p, b+1)
(p, b).i    = ((p, b).i, 0)
```

A fresh point that `new` creates inside the body of a recursive box is not one
constant. Each round creates a different channel. We read it as a family ν
whose index coordinates are the round counters of the enclosing boxes, so it is
again a shape with d_ν ≥ 1. The fresh lower point ℓ of E-Abs is treated the same
way. Frame variables are no points and never enter G.

#### 1.2  Unit schemas

A point term over counter variables κ₁, …, κ_m is a shape f together with one
coordinate expression per coordinate, each either a constant c ∈ ℕ or κ_j + c.
A schema is an atom between two point terms, together with the domain κ̄ ∈ ℕ^m.

```
χ  ::=  s ≺ s′  |  s ≡ s′            s, s′ point terms over κ̄,  κ̄ ∈ ℕ^m
```

A variable is shared when it occurs on both sides and free when it occurs on one
side only. The class L consists of finite sets of such schemas. Its instances are
the ground atoms obtained by κ̄ ∈ ℕ^m.

Diagonal freedom (D). No variable occurs twice on the same side.

(D) holds for every G that the forced invariants of §1.3 produce with one
counter per recursive box. A point term arises from one bound point variable by
carried roots and offsets, and the round counter of a box lands in one
coordinate of it. Distinct boxes contribute distinct counters.

Shifts. If κ is shared and stands at coordinate position j on the source as
κ + c and at position j′ on the target as κ + c′, the schema carries the value at
j to the value at j′ shifted by δ = c′ − c, and it exists only where the source
value is at least c.

#### 1.3  Where the schemas come from

An algorithmic checker emits the atoms its side conditions ask for. At top
level all points are ground, and the atoms enter G as facts and aliases. Inside
a box body the atoms mention the box's point variables and are entailments
under the hypotheses in scope (§7). Counter schemas enter G at the forces of
recursive boxes. A box whose rounds move its points by σ, with the facts C₀ of
one round, has the invariant

```
C = ⋃ₖ C₀[σᵏ(ιs)/ιs]
```

and a force at ground xs asks C₀ at σᵏ(xs) for every k ∈ ℕ. A box with
several recursive call sites σ₁, …, σ_m has the union over all compositions of
the σᵢ. When σ shifts each point variable ι_j by s_j ∈ {0, s}, the instances
are ι_j + s·κ for one counter κ ∈ ℕ. Each fact of C₀ then becomes one unit
schema after residue splitting (Lemma 4 in §5.3). Request and reply is of this
form, with C₀ = {ι ≺ ι′, ι′ ≺ ι+1} and σ = (ι+1, ι′+1). Forced at
((a,0), (b,0)) it gives

```
(a, κ) ≺ (b, κ)        (b, κ) ≺ (a, κ+1)                       s = 1
```

A constraint written with a counter of its own gives the same kind of schema.
The tail call's {ι+k ≺ ζ ∣ k ∈ ℕ} forced at ((a,0), (z,0)) gives
(a, κ) ≺ (z, 0), with κ free. A round that sends a channel, or acts in an inner
loop on a received one, puts the round counter into an index coordinate. This
gives the free-coordinate schemas.

```
(c, κ) ≺ (c, κ).1              sender gate, delegation in every round
(c, κ).1 + b ≺ (c, κ+1)        receiver loops on the received channel, b free
```

Invariants that do not fit L are listed in §10.

#### 1.4  Nesting

Programs nest carried roots without bound. The received chain of ORDERS-SPEC
§13 receives a channel in each round and continues on it.

```
Sch = μX. ?(∃κ. X@κ).Close
rch : □(∀ι. C. ∗ ⊸^⊥_⊤ (Sch@ι ⊸^{ι+1}_⊤ ∗))
rch = !(f = λc. let (d, c) = recv c in let () = close c in spawn (λw. let () = w in (force f) d))
C   = {ι.1ⁿ + 1 ≺ ι.1ⁿ⁺¹ ∣ n ∈ ℕ}          ι.1ⁿ takes the carried root n times
```

The body closes c at ι+1 while the spawned thunk holds d at ι.1, so it needs
ι+1 ≺ ι.1. The call moves ι to ι.1. Forced at (c,0), C asks π + 1 ≺ π.1 for
every π on the chain (c,0), (c,0).1, (c,0).1.1, … No finite set of unit schemas
over finitely many shapes contains these facts. The static system types the
program (ch_deadlock_free), and the fragment cannot express its invariant. This
is a gap in what the fragment expresses, and it says nothing about
decidability.

Within a finite G in L the shapes are finitely many by definition. Carried
congruence can still generate deeper derived aliases (§2). We close the shapes as
follows.

Sh(G) is the least set of shapes that contains every shape of G, is closed under
prefixes, and is closed under alias transfer. If some alias schema relates a point
term of shape g to one of shape h, then g.w ∈ Sh(G) iff h.w ∈ Sh(G), for every
sequence of positions w.

Finite shapes (S). Sh(G) is finite.

(S) fails for self-delegation, where the alias (d, κ) ≡ (d, κ).1 puts d, d.1,
d.1.1, … into one transfer class. Section 10 lists (S) as unproved to be
decidable in general. For every example of ORDERS-SPEC §13 whose invariant lies
in L, Sh(G) is the set of prefixes of the shapes of G.

### 2  Strictness as a cycle problem

Γ_G is the following infinite graph. Its vertices are the ground points of shapes
in Sh(G). It has a strict edge x → y for each instance x ≺ y of a ≺ schema and for
program order x → x+1. It has weak edges x → y and y → x for each instance
x ≡ y of an alias schema in the alias closure A(G) below.

A(G) contains, for every alias schema s ≡ s′ of G and every sequence of positions
w = i₁…i_k with shape(s).w ∈ Sh(G), the schema

```
(s + ν₀).i₁ + ν₁ … .i_k + ν_k   ≡   (s′ + ν₀).i₁ + ν₁ … .i_k + ν_k
```

with fresh shared variables ν₀, …, ν_k. The case k = 0 is the successor closure
s + ν₀ ≡ s′ + ν₀.

Let E be the equivalence generated by the instances of A(G). It relates points of
shapes in Sh(G). Write u.w + n for (u + n₁).i₁ + n₂ ⋯ .i_k + n with
w = n₁ i₁ … n_k i_k nonempty. The outside equivalence O is the equivalence on
points of shapes outside Sh(G) generated by the pairs u.w + n ≡ v.w + n with u E v
and u.w outside Sh(G). Then v.w lies outside Sh(G) as well, by alias transfer along the chain from u to v.
O relates points with equal offsets.

**Lemma 1 (alias closure).** ≡_{W_G} is E ∪ O. In particular ≡_{W_G} restricted to
points of shapes in Sh(G) is E.

*Proof.* The instances of A(G) and the generators of O are aliases of W_G by
successor and carried congruence. This gives E ∪ O ⊆ ≡_{W_G}. For the other inclusion
it suffices that E ∪ O is a congruence that contains the base aliases, because
≡_{W_G} is the least such relation on all points. E and O live on disjoint sets of
points, so E ∪ O is an equivalence. E contains the base aliases (k = 0, ν₀ = 0).

Successor. E is closed under +1, because the set of instances of A(G) is (shift
ν_k). The generators of O are closed under +1 (raise n), so O is.

Carried roots on E. Let x E y by a chain x = z₀, …, z_m = y. Each z_j is related
to x by a chain of alias schemas. If x.i has a shape in Sh(G), then each z_j.i has a shape
in Sh(G) by alias transfer, and the instance of A(G) with word w.i and ν_{k+1} = 0
relates z_j.i to z_{j+1}.i. So x.i E y.i. If x.i lies outside Sh(G), then
x.i O y.i by the generator with u = x, v = y, w = 0 i and n = 0.

Carried roots on O. Sh(G) is prefix closed, so every carried root of an outside
point lies outside. The image of a generator u.w + n ≡ v.w + n under .i is the
generator for the word w n i with offset 0. So .i maps chains of generators to
chains of generators, and O is closed under carried roots. ∎

**Theorem 1 (reduction).** W_G is strict iff Γ_G has no closed walk that contains a
strict edge.

*Proof.* The order of W_G is the transitive closure of the strict edges composed
with ≡ on both sides, read on ≡-classes. It is irreflexive iff no point x has
x ≺⁺ x modulo ≡, that is, iff there is no closed walk of strict edges and
≡-steps with at least one strict edge. By Lemma 1 a ≡-step between points of
Sh(G) is an E-step, that is, a path of weak edges, and a ≡-step at a point outside
Sh(G) is an O-step and stays outside. It remains to show that points outside Sh(G)
lie on no such walk. No ≡-step relates a point of Sh(G) to an outside point. No
strict edge leaves or enters the outside part except program order, which keeps the
shape, because every ≺ schema mentions shapes of G only. Inside the outside part, O
preserves the offset and program order raises it, so it carries no closed walk with
a strict edge. ∎

Theorem 1 does not use (S). (S) makes the static graph below finite.

The static graph 𝒢 is the finite multigraph whose vertices are the shapes of
Sh(G). Each ≺ schema and the program-order schema of each shape give a strict
edge, and each schema of A(G) gives two weak edges, one per direction. Every
closed walk of Γ_G projects to a closed walk of 𝒢.

### 3  Roles

Roles need every edge of 𝒢 to be a unit schema. A schema of A(G) adds ν₀ to the
offsets of s and s′. If a shared variable κ sits at the offset of s and at an index
coordinate of s′, the offset of s + ν₀ is κ + c + ν₀, a sum of two variables. The
alias (e, κ) ≡ (c, κ).1 gives (e, κ + ν₀) ≡ (c, κ).1 + ν₀, whose instances
relate (e, m) to (c, k).1 + (m − k) for all k ≤ m. No finite set of unit schemas
has these instances (docs/history/DECIDABILITY-REVIEW.md, G2). We exclude this
case.

Offset alignment (A). In every alias schema of G, the two offsets hold either
constants or one shared variable κ, as κ + c on one side and κ + c′ on the other.

(A) costs one scan over the alias schemas. It fails in two ways. A shared variable
sits at the offset on one side and at an index coordinate on the other, the case
above. Or an offset holds a free variable κ. Then the instances with κ = 0 and κ = 1
are aliased to the same point, so (f, …, c) ≡ (f, …, c+1), and program order makes
W_G non-strict. The second failure therefore decides the instance at once, and only
the first one rejects G. The first failure is delegation in every round of a
recursion, with a payload alias that carries the round counter. A forced ≺
invariant produces no alias schema (Lemma 9), so (A) rejects only G with counter
aliases of that kind.

Under (D) and (A) every schema of A(G) is a unit schema. ν₀ lands in the
coordinates that were the offsets of s and s′, and ν_k for k ≥ 1 in fresh offsets.
If those coordinates hold κ + c and κ + c′, κ occurs nowhere else by (D), and κ + ν₀
renames to one variable κ′ ∈ ℕ with the same instances. Otherwise they hold
constants, and c + ν₀, c′ + ν₀ are unit expressions with ν₀ shared. (D) carries over
to A(G).

Union the coordinate positions (f, j) over all edges of 𝒢 whenever one shared
variable sits at (f, j) on one side and at (g, j′) on the other. The classes are
roles.

Role consistency (R). No role contains two positions of the same shape.

Under (R) every vertex (f, x) has at most one r-coordinate x_r for each role r,
and R(f) denotes the roles present at f. For an edge e and a role r, e shares r
when a shared variable carries r from source to target, with shift δ_e[r] ∈ ℤ, and
when r is absent at both ends, with shift 0. Otherwise e cuts r. An edge cuts r when
r is present on one side only, when it holds a free variable on one side, or when it
holds a constant on one side.

(R) and (D) are checked by one union-find pass over G. Program order shares every
role of its shape, with shift 1 on the offset role and 0 elsewhere. Under (A) the
schemas of A(G) share the roles of s and s′ and the fresh roles ν_k.

**Lemma 2 (threads).** Assume (D), (R) and (A) and that no schema of G contains a
constant. Then Γ_G has a closed walk with a strict edge iff 𝒢 has a closed walk
e₁ … e_n with a strict edge such that every role r is cut by some e_k or satisfies
Σ_k δ_{e_k}[r] = 0.

*Proof.* (⇒) Project the concrete walk to 𝒢. If no edge cuts r, every edge
carries the r-coordinate and adds its shift, and the walk returns to its start, so
the shifts sum to 0.

(⇐) Let C bound every constant of G and Δ bound every |δ|, and put
N = C + nΔ + 1. Assign concrete r-coordinates along the walk role by role. If no
edge cuts r, start at N and add the shifts. The values stay in [N − nΔ, N + nΔ],
above every guard, and return to N. If some edge cuts r, give r the value N at
the target of every cutting edge that has r there, and carry it forward by the
shifts until the next cutting edge, cyclically. Values again stay above every
guard. Every edge instance is now determined. A shared variable takes the source
value minus its constant, which is ≥ 0. A free variable takes whatever its side
needs. (D) makes these choices independent and (R) makes "the r-coordinate" of
each vertex well defined. The walk closes because each role returns to its
starting value. ∎

Lemma 2 is where coordinates become independent threads. Its proof uses the
absence of constants twice, once for "start anywhere" and once for "shift up".
Section 5 removes constants on monotone roles. Section 6 shows that general
constants make the problem undecidable.

### 4  Uniform and free-coordinate edges

A walk of 𝒢 is valid when it satisfies the condition of Lemma 2. For a subgraph
H of 𝒢, cut(H) is the set of roles cut by some edge of H. For a role set S, LP_S(H, e)
is the linear program over x ∈ ℚ^{E(H)}

```
Σ_{e′ ∈ in(v)} x_{e′} = Σ_{e′ ∈ out(v)} x_{e′}     for each vertex v of H
x ≥ 0
Σ_{e′} x_{e′} δ_{e′}[r] = 0                          for each role r ∉ S
x_e ≥ 1
```

This is Kosaraju and Sullivan's multicycle LP (their Thm 2.4, p. 399, in the
per-edge form x_e ≥ 1 of their §3, p. 400) with the labels restricted to the roles outside S. A feasible x scales to an integral
circulation whose support contains e.

**Algorithm SFZ(H)** (strict zero cycles with free coordinates).

```
1  S := cut(H)
2  loop
3     H := H without the edges that cut a role outside S
4     E′ := { e ∈ H | LP_S(H, e) feasible }
5     if E′ has no strict edge: return No
6     if S ⊄ cut(E′): S := S ∩ cut(E′); continue
7     let Q₁, …, Q_k be the strongly connected components of (V(H), E′)
8     if k = 1: return Yes
9     return SFZ(H|Q₁) ∨ … ∨ SFZ(H|Q_k)
```

Singleton vertices without E′ edges count as components. H|Q keeps the edges of
H with both ends in Q.

**Theorem 3 (free coordinates).** Under (D), (R), (A) and (S), for constant-free
G, SFZ(𝒢) returns Yes iff Γ_G has a closed walk with a strict edge, and it runs in
polynomial time.

*Proof.* By Lemma 2 it suffices to show that SFZ(H) returns Yes iff H contains a
valid closed walk.

Invariant. Every valid walk W contained in the input H stays inside H at line 3
and inside E′ at line 4. Let S(W) be the roles W cuts. Initially S(W) ⊆ cut(H) = S.
If S(W) ⊆ S, line 3 removes no edge of W. W is a circulation with zero sum on each
role outside S(W), hence on each role outside S, so its edge counts are feasible
for LP_S(H, e) for each e on W, and W ⊆ E′. Then S(W) ⊆ cut(E′), and line 6
keeps S(W) ⊆ S.

No at line 5. A valid W has a strict edge and lies in E′.

Recursion at line 9. A valid W is a closed walk inside E′, so its vertices lie in
one component Q, and W ⊆ H|Q.

Yes at line 8. Every vertex of H lies in the one component, so E′ spans V(H) and
is strongly connected. For each e ∈ E′ take an integral solution x^e of
LP_S(H, e). Its support lies in E′, because x^e also witnesses feasibility for
every edge of its support. The sum x = Σ_e x^e is a circulation with support
exactly E′ and zero sum on every role outside S. Its multigraph is balanced and
connected, so it has an Euler circuit W (Kosaraju–Sullivan, proof of Thm 2.2,
p. 399). W uses every edge of E′. So W contains a strict edge (line 5 passed) and
cuts every role in S (line 6 passed). Every role outside S is shared by all edges
of H (line 3) and has zero sum on W. So W is valid.

Termination and cost. Line 6 shrinks S, so the loop runs at most |roles| + 1
times per call. Line 9 recurses only when k ≥ 2, on smaller vertex sets. As in
Kosaraju–Sullivan (Thm 3.3 and its proof, p. 401), the calls at one recursion depth work on
disjoint subgraphs, and the depth is at most |V(𝒢)|. The total is
O(|V| · (|roles| + 1) · |E|) feasibility tests of linear programs with |E|
variables and |V| + |E| + |roles| constraints, each polynomial. ∎

**Theorem 2 (uniform schemas).** Assume (D), (R), (A) and (S). If every edge
shares every role of both its ends (no free variables, no constants, equal role
sets), then S = ∅ throughout,
SFZ is Kosaraju and Sullivan's ZCYCLE with the extra requirement of a strict edge,
and strictness of W_G is decided in O(|V| · |E|) LP tests.

The strict-edge requirement is the only change to ZCYCLE. ZCYCLE asks for a zero
cycle, and strictness asks for a zero cycle through a strict edge. Weak edges
alone form zero cycles (an alias and its reverse), and those cycles are harmless.
The domain ℕ instead of ℤ costs nothing (the shift-up step in Lemma 2). S = ∅
relies on the convention of §3 that an edge shares, with shift 0, every role absent
at both its ends. Without it, weak components of 𝒢 with different role sets put
those roles into cut(𝒢). The answer is the same, because those roles have no shift
on those edges and their LP constraint is trivial
(docs/history/DECIDABILITY-REVIEW.md, G3).

### 5  Constants

#### 5.1  Monotone roles

A role carries a constant when some schema of A(G) holds a constant at a position
of that role. Carried points always carry one (the offset 0 of ι.i), and top-level
facts carry only constants.

The shift graph of a role r has the shapes in which r is present as vertices and
an edge f → g of weight δ_e[r] for every edge e of 𝒢 that shares r. Program order
contributes a self-loop of weight 1 on the offset role. An alias contributes
weights δ and −δ.

Monotone boundaries (M). The shift graph of every role that carries a constant
has no negative cycle.

(M) is checked by Bellman–Ford. If it holds, take shortest-path potentials π_r
with δ_e[r] + π_r(src e) − π_r(tgt e) ≥ 0 and replace each r-coordinate x_r at
shape f by x_r − π_r(f). Shifts become nonnegative, and constants and guards move
by bounded amounts. Below, assume every shift on a constant-carrying role is ≥ 0.
Let L and B be the least and greatest constant or guard on such roles after the
change.

#### 5.2  Folding monotone roles into the control

Let R_c be the roles that carry a constant and A = {L, …, B, ∞}. The abstract graph
𝒢^# has vertices (f, a) with a assigning a value of A to each role of R_c present
at f. It has an edge (f, a) → (g, a′) for an edge e of 𝒢 when for each r ∈ R_c

```
e shares r              a′_r = a_r ⊕ δ_e[r]   and a_r ≥ the guard     (⊕ saturates above B to ∞)
constant c at source    a_r = c
constant c at target    a′_r = c
free at source          a_r ≥ the guard
free at target          a′_r ≥ the guard, any value of A
```

The edge keeps e's strictness and e's shifts on roles outside R_c. For r ∈ R_c it
shares r with shift δ_e[r] when e shares r and a_r = a′_r = ∞. In every other case
it cuts r. 𝒢^# is constant-free.

**Lemma 3 (abstraction).** Under (D), (R), (A) and (M), Γ_G has a closed walk with a
strict edge iff 𝒢^# has a valid closed walk.

*Proof.* (⇒) Map each concrete vertex (f, x) to (f, a) with a_r = x_r when
x_r ≤ B and ∞ otherwise. Saturating addition abstracts a nonnegative shift
exactly, so each concrete edge maps to an edge of 𝒢^#. For a role outside R_c the
condition of Lemma 2 holds as before. A role r ∈ R_c not cut in 𝒢^# is shared at ∞
by every edge, so the concrete values exceed B everywhere and the shifts sum to 0.

(⇐) Roles outside R_c are assigned as in Lemma 2. Take r ∈ R_c. If 𝒢^# does not
cut r on the walk, every edge shares r at ∞ and the shifts sum to 0, so start at
max(N, B+1) and carry. Otherwise split the walk cyclically at the edges of 𝒢 that
cut r. A segment starts at a constant (exact value) or a free variable (choose the
abstract value, or a large value for ∞). Inside a segment the finite abstract
values are exact, and once the abstract value reaches ∞ the concrete value is above
B and stays consistent with all later guards and tests, which all lie at or below
B. If no edge of 𝒢 cuts r but 𝒢^# does, then some abstract value on the walk is
finite. Values are nondecreasing around the walk and return to their start, so
they are all equal and all shifts on r are 0. The concrete value is that constant.
In each case the concrete walk closes. ∎

**Theorem 4 (boundaries on monotone roles).** Under (D), (R), (A), (S) and (M),
strictness of W_G is decidable. Run SFZ on 𝒢^#. The size of 𝒢^# is at most
|Sh(G)| · (B − L + 2)^ρ, with ρ the largest number of constant-carrying roles at
one shape.

*Proof.* Theorem 1, Lemma 3 and Theorem 3. ∎

Request and reply, delegation, the scheduler and bad link satisfy (M). In
request and reply the shifts are 0 and 1. In delegation the offset role of c.1
carries the constants 0 and j, and its shifts are program order (+1) and the
payload alias (0). In the scheduler and bad link every constant sits on a cut,
and the shared shifts come from program order and aliases with equal offsets.
The tail call's schema (a, κ) ≺ (z, 0) has κ free and shares no role, so its
shift graphs hold program order only. The relay violates (M) (§9.2).

#### 5.3  Coefficients other than 1

Rounds that shift by s > 1 give coordinate expressions s·κ + c. Extend point terms
by such coefficients.

Equal coefficients (C). In every schema, a shared variable has the same
coefficient at its two occurrences.

**Lemma 4 (residue splitting).** Assume (C). For each role r let m_r be the least
common multiple of the coefficients at positions of r. Write each r-coordinate as
x = m_r·q + ρ with 0 ≤ ρ < m_r and move ρ into the shape. Then G becomes an
equivalent set of unit schemas over the split shapes, and (D), (R) and (M) carry
over.

*Proof.* A variable κ with coefficient s occurs at positions of one role r, and
s | m_r. Substitute κ = (m_r/s)·κ′ + t for each t with 0 ≤ t < m_r/s. An
occurrence s·κ + c becomes m_r·κ′ + (s·t + c), with quotient κ′ + ⌊(s·t + c)/m_r⌋
and remainder (s·t + c) mod m_r. Each t gives one unit schema in κ′ ∈ ℕ, and the
union over t has the same instances as the original. A constant c becomes the
quotient ⌊c/m_r⌋ with remainder c mod m_r. Program order κ + 1 is the case s = 1.
The substitution keeps every variable at its positions, so (D) and (R) hold. A
closed walk of the split shift graph returns to its residues and projects to a
closed walk of the original whose total shift is m_r times the split total, so
negative cycles correspond and (M) holds. ∎

The split multiplies the number of shapes by at most Π_r m_r. Schemas that violate
(C), such as (a, 2κ) ≺ (b, κ), are scalings and not shifts (§10).

### 6  Undecidability with general boundaries

**Theorem 5.** Strictness of W_G is undecidable for finite sets of unit schemas
that satisfy (D), (R), (A) and (S), use no alias schemas, and use shapes of depth
at most 3. The roles that carry constants then violate (M).

*Proof.* Reduce halting of a deterministic two-counter machine M from (q₀, 0, 0)
(Minsky 1961). Encode a configuration (q, x, y) at clock t as the point

```
⟨q; t, x, y⟩  ≔  (((a, t).q + x).1 + y).1
```

with states as positions. The machine edges advance the clock.

```
inc x, q → q′          ⟨q; κt, κx, κy⟩      ≺  ⟨q′; κt+1, κx+1, κy⟩
x = 0, q → q′          ⟨q; κt, 0, κy⟩       ≺  ⟨q′; κt+1, 0, κy⟩
x > 0, dec x, q → q″   ⟨q; κt, κx+1, κy⟩    ≺  ⟨q″; κt+1, κx, κy⟩
```

and likewise for y. At the halting state h a cleanup phase empties x, then y,
then t, without advancing the clock, and closes the loop at the start.

```
⟨h; κt, κx, κy⟩       ≺  ⟨h_x; κt, κx, κy⟩
⟨h_x; κt, κx+1, κy⟩   ≺  ⟨h_x; κt, κx, κy⟩       ⟨h_x; κt, 0, κy⟩  ≺  ⟨h_y; κt, 0, κy⟩
⟨h_y; κt, 0, κy+1⟩    ≺  ⟨h_y; κt, 0, κy⟩        ⟨h_y; κt, 0, 0⟩   ≺  ⟨h_t; κt, 0, 0⟩
⟨h_t; κt+1, 0, 0⟩     ≺  ⟨h_t; κt, 0, 0⟩
⟨h_t; 0, 0, 0⟩        ≺  ⟨q₀; 0, 0, 0⟩
```

Every schema has offset 0 on both sides. Program order leaves offset 0 for vertices
that no schema touches, and from there only program order continues, so program
order lies on no cycle. Prefix shapes carry only program order. There are no
aliases.

A cycle avoiding the last edge stays in the machine phase, where t strictly
increases, or in the cleanup phase, where x + y + t strictly decreases or the
phase index increases. So every cycle passes ⟨q₀; 0, 0, 0⟩. From there the machine
edges follow the run of M deterministically, because the zero branch requires
x = 0 and the decrement branch x ≥ 1. The cleanup succeeds from every halting
configuration. Hence W_G is non-strict iff M halts. ∎

Forced invariants produce each ingredient of the construction on its own. These
are shared counters with shifts ±1 between carried families (lockstep loops over
two received channels), constants at carried offsets and at top level, and
positions as distinct families. Whether one well-typed program produces the whole
gadget is open (§9.3).

### 7  Box bodies and ground queries

#### 7.1  The setting

An algorithmic checker types a program at one well-formed world W = W_G, and W
knows constants only (ORDERS-SPEC §9). A box body □(∀ιs. C. ∗ ⊸^u_⊤ T) is typed
once, with ιs as point variables and the hypotheses of the enclosing boxes
followed by (C, ιs). Every side condition reads the fact theory Γ ⊢ of
ORDERS-SPEC §9. This section treats hypotheses with finitely many ≺ facts. A
recursive call whose instance of C is contained in C needs no entailment at all,
because the body's own hypothesis gives every fact. For an invariant
⋃ₖ C₀[σᵏ(ιs)/ιs] and a call at σ(ιs) the containment holds by construction.

A side condition is ground when all its points have constant roots and open
otherwise. A ground condition can enter W as a fact or an alias. An open
condition cannot, because W relates a point with a variable root only by
reflexivity and program order (sharp edges 57). With ≺ hypotheses only, Γ ⊢ ≡ is
≡_W, and between open points it is syntactic equality. G is therefore a finite
set of ground atoms and ground aliases together with the forced schemas of §8.

The checker decides three things. W_G is strict. Every open condition is entailed
by W_G and the hypotheses in scope. And G is chosen so that both hold (Lemma 7).

#### 7.2  Entailment and ground queries

**Lemma 5 (entailment).** Let G be ground, W = W_G, Ψ a finite set of ≺ facts over
point terms, and x, y point terms. Let N consist of x, y and the endpoints of Ψ, so
|N| ≤ 2|Ψ| + 2. The graph H on N has a strict edge u → v when u ≺ v ∈ Ψ, when u and v
have constant roots and u ≺_W v, or when u and v lie in one family with a variable
root and u has the smaller offset. It has a weak edge u → v when u ≡_W v. Then
Γ ⊢ x ≺ y holds iff H has a path from x to y with a strict edge.

*Proof.* A derivation of Γ ⊢ x ≺ y is a chain x = z₀, …, z_n = y whose steps
are ≺_W steps or Ψ facts, each up to ≡_W. Cut the chain at the endpoints of its Ψ
steps. Between two cuts every step is a ≺_W step, and these compose into one ≺_W
step by transitivity. ≺_W relates points with constant roots by the order W_G
generates, points of one family with a variable root by program order, and nothing
else. So every segment is an edge of H. Conversely every edge of H is a step of
Γ ⊢ ≺. ∎

Lemma 5 costs at most |N|² ground queries u ≺_W v and u ≡_W v and one search in H.
Lemma 6 reduces both queries to strictness.

**Lemma 6 (ground queries).** Let W_G be strict and x, y ground. Then x ≼_{W_G} y iff
W_{G ∪ {y ≺ x}} is not strict. Further x ≡_{W_G} y iff x ≼ y and y ≼ x, and
x ≺_{W_G} y iff x ≼ y and not y ≼ x.

*Proof.* ORDERS-SPEC §5 has successor and carried congruence for ≡ only, so the new
fact leaves ≡ unchanged and adds the single edge y → x to Γ_G. A closed walk with a
strict edge must use it, because W_G is strict. Between two uses it runs from x to
y in Γ_G, which gives x ≼_{W_G} y. Conversely x ≼ y closes such a walk. The other
two claims hold because a strict W_G never has x ≺ y and y ≼ x together. ∎

G ∪ {y ≺ x} is ground. It can add shapes to Sh(G) and make a role carry a constant,
so the hypotheses of Theorem 4 must be checked again for it.

**Lemma 7 (choosing G).** Let G₀ hold the ground conditions, and let P be the finite
set of ground points that occur in the open conditions and in the hypotheses in
scope. Some G ⊇ G₀ with W_G strict entails every open condition iff some
G₀ ∪ R does, where R is a set of facts and aliases between points of P.

*Proof.* Take a strict W′ ⊇ W_{G₀} that entails every open condition. Let R be the
facts a ≺ b and aliases a ≡ b with a, b ∈ P that hold in W′. W_{G₀ ∪ R} ⊑ W′, so it
is strict. By Lemma 5 each open condition needs only ≺_W and ≡_W between points of
P, and R supplies those. ∎

The search ranges over relations on P, finitely many, and each candidate costs one
strictness test and the entailments of Lemma 5. This bound is exponential in |P|. A
better one is open.

### 8  Forced constraints and ground G

#### 8.1  The force obligation

A force at ground points xs outside every box asks every fact of C[xs/ιs] in W_G.
Let M be the set of maps that the recursive calls generate, {σᵏ ∣ k ∈ ℕ} for one
call site at σ and all compositions of σ₁, …, σ_m for m call sites. An invariant
C = ⋃_{τ ∈ M} C₀[τ(ιs)/ιs] with ≺ facts then asks

```
C(xs)  =  { x[τ(xs)/ιs] ≺ y[τ(xs)/ιs]  |  x ≺ y ∈ C₀,  τ ∈ M }
```

A force of a recursive box inside another body at open points asks an infinite
family of open entailments. This document does not decide it.

**Lemma 8 (force obligation).** Let G₀ hold the ground conditions and let F be the
union of C(xs) over all outside force sites. Some strict W ⊇ W_{G₀} entails F and
every open condition iff W_{G₀ ∪ R ∪ F} is strict for some R as in Lemma 7.

*Proof.* Every world that entails F contains W_{G₀ ∪ R ∪ F}, with R read off it as in
Lemma 7. Conversely W_{G₀ ∪ R ∪ F} has F as facts. ∎

So the obligation for every τ ∈ M becomes strictness of one schema set. Lemma 6 is
still the tool for the finitely many ground queries of Lemma 5. The world generated
by F depends on the set F only, so each fact of C₀ contributes its own family with
its own counters.

**Lemma 9 (no alias schemas).** If every forced constraint has ≺ facts only, F has
≺ schemas only. The aliases of G₀ ∪ R are ground. So every alias schema of the
final G is ground, and (D), (C) and (A) hold for the alias part as in §8.2.

Consequently G2 cannot arise for ≺ invariants. (A) restricts alias schemas only,
and a ≺ schema may carry a counter at an offset on one side and at an index
coordinate on the other. The relay schemas of §9.2 are of that kind and lie in L.

Constraints with ≡ facts add alias schemas. The forwarder's constraint, forced at
((a,0), (b,0)), gives (a, κ).1 ≡ (b, κ).1. Its offsets hold the constant 0 on both
sides, so it satisfies (A), and it relates shapes of equal depth. We have not
checked (R) and (M) for the forwarder's whole G.

#### 8.2  Strictness for ground G

Ground G lies in L with m = 0. The schemas of A(G) contain only the fresh variables
ν_k, each once per side, at the offsets or at the index coordinates the offsets
become. So (D), (C) and (A) hold, and Lemma 4 is not needed. Lemma 1 and Theorem 1
apply. (R) and (S) hold when every alias relates shapes of equal depth (§10.3).
Theorem 4 then decides strictness under (M).

Delegation breaks both premises. The payload alias x ≡ ι.i relates the shape of x
to a shape one carried root deeper than that of ι, so aliases of unequal depth are
the rule. (M) also fails for a plain program. A process receives d on c at instant
2, acts twice on d, and sends d on c at instant 7. The payload alias is
(c,2).1 + 2 ≡ (c,7).1, and A(G) holds (c,2).1 + (2 + ν₀) ≡ (c,7).1 + ν₀. Its
shift on the offset role of c.1 is −2 in one direction. Any ground fact at an
offset of c.1 makes that role carry a constant, so the shift graph has a negative
cycle. The cycle is not a closed walk of Γ_G, because the index coordinates 2 and
7 are distinct constants. So (M) rejects this G although no closed walk realizes
the negative cycle. A criterion that looks only at the walks Lemma 3 must realize
is open.

### 9  Classes of invariants

The forced families of §8.1 depend on how the recursive calls move the box's
points. This section sorts invariants by their maps and names the lemma each
class needs. Lemmas 8 and 9 are proved. The rest says whether we have the lemma.

#### 9.1  The three classes

**(a) Pure shifts.** σ_i(ι_j) = ι_j + s_ij. The maps commute, a composition reduces
to its count vector n ∈ ℕ^m, and the root ι_j moves by s_j · n. A fact rooted at
ι_j and ι_j′ has one counter coordinate per side, at the offset or at the first
index coordinate. Its coordinate pairs form the monoid generated by
g_i = (s_ij, s_ij′) ⊆ ℕ².

- Every g_i diagonal (s_ij = s_ij′). Unit schemas with equal coefficients after
  Lemma 4 and finitely many ground exceptions. Theorems 2 to 4 apply under (R), (S)
  and (M). Request and reply is here. σ = (ι+1, ι′+1) gives (a, κ) ≺ (b, κ) and
  (b, κ) ≺ (a, κ+1), shifts 0 and 1, so (M) holds and Theorem 4 decides it.
- Every g_i on an axis. Free coordinates. Theorems 3 and 4 apply. The tail call
  is here, with the ceiling ζ fixed and ι shifted.
- Diagonal and axis generators mixed. One side holds κ + κ′ with κ′ free, so the
  edge shares a role with shift set c + ℕ, c − ℕ or ℤ. Needed is Lemma 2⁺, Lemma 2
  for interval shifts. SFZ extends by one LP variable y_{e,r} per interval edge and
  role with y ≥ c·x_e or y ≤ c·x_e, which keeps the LP homogeneous. In (M) an edge
  c − ℕ or ℤ counts as an unbounded negative weight. We expect the proofs of
  Lemma 2, Theorem 3 and Lemma 3 to go through with these changes. Not written out.
- A generator off the diagonal and off the axes. A scaling, open as §10.4.

**(b) One map that moves into carried roots.** Let j → j′ when σ(ι_j) is rooted at
ι_j′. The depth of σ^k(xs) is bounded iff no cycle of this functional graph
contains a carried entry. Without such cycles, after a finite tail and residue
splitting by the cycle lengths, each root carries one counter at one coordinate.
Each fact then gives unit schemas with one counter per side and finitely many
ground instances. Equal cycle shifts give (C), and Lemma 4 puts the family into L.
No new lemma is needed, and the class reduces to Theorems 2 to 4 under (R), (S)
and (M). The relay is here, and it violates (M) (§9.2).

**(c) Several maps that do not commute.** Compositions form a tree. Let U be the
union of the dependency graphs of the maps. A root's chain under a composition is
a path of U, so depth is unbounded iff U has a reachable cycle through a carried
entry. This test is decidable.

- Unbounded depth. The instances are points with ever longer carried paths, as in
  §1.4. The graph is a prefix-rewriting graph whose letters carry counters
  (§10.2). Open.
- Bounded depth. The pair of chains of a fact is a path of the product of U with
  itself. Its coordinate vectors form a finite union of linear sets, from the
  decomposition of a path into a simple path and simple cycles. Each generator adds
  a cycle's shift to one coordinate per side. A component whose generators each hit
  distinct coordinates reduces as in (a). A coordinate hit by two diagonal
  generators that lead to two different coordinates of the other side holds a sum
  r = r₁ + r₂ of two roles. This is the ≺ form of G2. Lemma 2 fails exactly in its
  (⇐) step, where (D) and (R) let each role be assigned on its own. A sum edge ties
  two threads at one position of the walk, so validity depends on prefix sums and
  not on edge counts alone. Open.

#### 9.2  The relay violates (M)

The relay of ORDERS-SPEC §13 holds the channel it received in the previous round
while it receives the next one, and it acts on the new channel first. The variant
below recurses by a tail call under a ceiling ζ in place of spawn, and it runs
after one round t₀ of its own. Its schemas are the smallest that show the effect.
We have not checked the variant in Rocq.

```
D   = ?∗.Close
S   = μX. ?(∃ι. D@ι). X

r   : □(∀ι ι′ ζ. C. ∗ ⊸^⊥_⊤ (S@ι ⊗ Close@ι′ ⊸^ζ_⊤ ∗))
r   = !(f = λp. let (c, d) = p in
               let (e, c) = recv c in
               let (v, e) = recv e in let () = v in
               let () = close d in
               (force f) ⟨c, e⟩)

t₀  = let (d, c) = recv c in let (v, d) = recv d in let () = v in (force r) ⟨c, d⟩

C₀  = {ι ≺ ι′, ι.1 ≺ ι′, ι.1 ≺ ι+1, ι′ ≺ ι+1, ι′ ≺ ι.1+1, ι ≺ ζ, ι.1 ≺ ζ, ι′ ≺ ζ}
σ   = (ι+1, ι.1+1, ζ)          C = ⋃ₖ C₀[σᵏ(ι, ι′, ζ)/(ι, ι′, ζ)]
```

The two receives block at ι and at ι.1, and close d blocks at ι′. Each must lie
below everything the body holds at that moment and below the ceiling, which
gives the facts of C₀. The call at σ(ι, ι′, ζ) has instance C₀[σ] ∪ …, which is
contained in C.

P-Term types t₀ as an open process with Σ = c : S@(c, 0), where the point constant
is written c as in ORDERS-SPEC §13. We have not written out the partner. t₀ forces
r at ((c, 1), (c, 0).1 + 1, (z, 0)) for a point constant z that no channel uses.

Write c_k = (c, k) and d_k^j = (c, k).1 + j. Round k ≥ 1 runs at the instance
(c_k, d_{k−1}^1, (z, 0)), the image of the first instance under σ^{k−1}. Round k
contributes the following facts.

```
recv c     c_k ≺ d_{k−1}^1                              c_k ≺ (z, 0)
recv e     d_k^0 ≺ d_{k−1}^1,    d_k^0 ≺ c_{k+1}         d_k^0 ≺ (z, 0)
close d    d_{k−1}^1 ≺ c_{k+1},  d_{k−1}^1 ≺ d_k^1        d_{k−1}^1 ≺ (z, 0)
```

t₀ adds d_0^0 ≺ c_1. With k = κ+1 the round facts are unit schemas over one
counter κ ∈ ℕ.

```
(c, κ+1) ≺ (c, κ).1 + 1
(c, κ+1).1 ≺ (c, κ).1 + 1          (c, κ+1).1 ≺ (c, κ+2)
(c, κ).1 + 1 ≺ (c, κ+2)            (c, κ).1 + 1 ≺ (c, κ+1).1 + 1
(c, κ+1) ≺ (z, 0)     (c, κ+1).1 ≺ (z, 0)     (c, κ).1 + 1 ≺ (z, 0)
(c, 0).1 ≺ (c, 1)                                                      ground, from t₀
```

**Claim.** W_G is strict, G satisfies (D), (R), (C) and (S), and G violates (M).

*Proof.* Rank c_k by 3k, d_k^0 by 3k+1, d_k^j for j ≥ 1 by 3k+6−1/j, and (z, j) by
ω+j. Every fact and every program-order step on these points raises the rank. Every
other point carries program order only. G has no alias schema, so W_G is strict.

Each schema has κ at most once per side, with coefficient 1, so (D) and (C) hold.
Without alias schemas Sh(G) is the set of prefixes {c, c.1, z}, so (S) holds. The
first schema puts κ at the offset of c and at the index coordinate of c.1, and every
other shared κ sits at one of these two positions. So the role r consists of these
two positions, one per shape, and (R) holds. The offsets of c.1 and of z hold only
constants and form roles of their own.

The ground fact (c, 0).1 ≺ (c, 1) puts constants at both positions of r, so r
carries a constant. The schema (c, κ+1).1 ≺ (c, κ).1 + 1 shares r with shift −1
and gives the shift graph of r a self-loop of weight −1 at c.1. ∎

The relay of ORDERS-SPEC §13 (rel_deadlock_free) recurses through spawn, so its C₀
has no ceiling facts, and its producer adds ι ≺ ι.1. Forced at ((a,0), (b,0)) it
gives the schemas above without the three ceiling schemas, plus
(a, κ+1) ≺ (a, κ+1).1, with the ground facts of round 0 in place of t₀. The added
schema shares κ between the same two positions with shift 0. The argument for
(D), (R), (C) and (S) and for the violation of (M) therefore carries over, and the
world of rel_deadlock_free is strict.

So Theorem 4 does not cover the relay. Its family c.1 has one counting coordinate,
so the relay falls in the open case of §10.1.

#### 9.3  The gadget

The relay realizes one ingredient of Theorem 5 that §6 does not list, a role that
carries a constant and has a negative shift. It is the cleanup edge
⟨h_t; κt+1, 0, 0⟩ ≺ ⟨h_t; κt, 0, 0⟩ in one coordinate. It does not realize the
gadget. Four pieces are missing.

1. Three counting coordinates in one family. The gadget uses points
   (((a, t).q + x).1 + y).1 whose index coordinates t, x and y move independently.
   Each counter comes from a recursion. This needs three nested recursive boxes
   whose bodies act on channels received at depth 3 and hold the channel of the
   previous round at each level, as the relay does at depth 1.
2. Zero test and decrement at one coordinate. The zero branch needs a schema with
   the constant 0 at x while t and y are shared. The first action on a freshly
   received channel produces such constants. The decrement needs x shared between
   the channels of rounds t and t+1 with shift −1, from a lockstep inner loop over
   two received channels. An invariant states the facts of both code paths in every
   round. This matches the gadget only if the facts of each path realize exactly
   its edge.
3. No extra cycles. Each blocking action asks a fact towards every held head and
   towards the ceiling, and each send asks ι ≺ ι.i. A gadget edge may become a
   chain of such facts. A reduction must show that no combination of these facts
   closes a cycle that the run of M does not close.
4. A computable map M ↦ P_M with P_M well typed iff M does not halt. With pieces 1
   to 3 this makes type checking over the fragment undecidable, beyond the
   undecidability of strictness for arbitrary schema sets.

Typability over the fragment does not imply (M). Decidable type checking then
needs one of three things. (M) becomes a premise of the algorithmic system, or a
procedure decides the forced sets beyond Theorem 4, or a proof shows that programs
never produce the gadget.

#### 9.4  What a decision procedure needs

1. Lemma 8 and Lemma 9, proved here.
2. For (a), Lemma 2⁺ with SFZ and Lemma 3 extended to interval shifts. Planned, not
   proved. Scalings stay open.
3. For (b), nothing new beyond the open case §10.1, which the relay hits.
4. For (c), the depth test, which is decidable. With bounded depth and no sum
   coordinates, as (a). Sum coordinates and unbounded depth are open.
5. Forces of recursive boxes at open points inside box bodies, and body
   entailments under counter hypotheses. Open.

### 10  What does not go through

1. Boundary facts do not reduce by unrolling in general (Theorem 5). They do for
   monotone roles in any dimension (Theorem 4). The case of non-monotone boundary
   roles with fewer than three counting coordinates per family is open. The
   criterion (M) is syntactic and cheap. The relay violates it (§9.2), and so does
   a ground G with a delegation round trip (§8.2). Theorem 5 says nothing about
   ground G.
2. Carried roots nest without bound in programs (§1.4). The fragment cannot express
   the received chain's invariant. A schema language with variables ranging over
   names would cover it. Its graphs are prefix-rewriting graphs with counters, and
   nothing here decides them.
3. (S), finiteness of the shape closure, is defined but its decidability in
   general is not proved here. It holds when every alias schema relates two shapes
   of equal depth, because transfer then preserves depth and the positions come
   from G. Delegation emits aliases of unequal depth, also in ground G (§8.2).
4. Invariants outside L. A box whose points move at different rates (a sent twice
   per round, b once) gives schemas such as (a, 2κ) ≺ (b, κ), a scaling and not a
   shift. Lemma 4 does not apply. A consistent choice of rates per position would
   rescale them, but we have not written that case out. A cycle with rate product
   ≠ 1 gives affine maps with nontrivial multipliers, and their fixed points can
   close cycles. Several recursive call sites with different shift vectors give
   coordinates that are sums of counters. Both cases are open.
5. Violations of (R). A schema such as (a, κ₁).1 + κ₂ ≺ (a, κ₂).1 + κ₁ permutes
   coordinates. Without free variables and constants, lifting shapes to (shape,
   permutation) pairs reduces it to Theorem 2, at a factor d!. With free variables
   the lifting interacts with cuts, and that case is open.
6. Offset alignment (A). We did not extend Lemma 2 to edges that relate an offset
   on one side to the sum of an index and an offset on the other. Such an edge
   relates (e, m) to (c, k).1 + (m − k) for all k ≤ m, a two-variable constraint
   that roles cannot express. (A) rejects these G. A forced ≺ invariant never
   produces them (Lemma 9).
7. Ground G. Open are strictness for ground G with aliases of unequal depth or
   with roles that violate (M), a polynomial bound for the choice of G in Lemma 7,
   and whether ground G can encode a two-counter machine through A(G).
8. Constraints with ≡ facts and body entailments under counter hypotheses. Lemma 5
   assumes finitely many ≺ hypotheses, and Lemma 9 assumes ≺ invariants. The
   forwarder needs both extensions (§8.1).
9. The fragment itself. ORDERS-SPEC names the fragment in one sentence. Section 1
   fixes one class of schemas, and a different choice changes every result.
