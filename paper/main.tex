\begin{abstract}
  % Deadlock freedom is a fundamental correctness property of concurrent systems.
  Due to its global nature, deadlock freedom is hard to ensure compositionally.
  Session Types based on a Curry-Howard correspondence with Linear Logic
  achieve compositional deadlock freedom by imposing quite stringent
  constraints on the communication topologies that can be constructed.
  All soundness proofs of such session type systems in the literature
  hinge on topological constraints, making it impossible to safely compose
  typable processes with processes that violate these constraints, but
  which are safe nevertheless.
  
  In this work, we present a new account of session types
  through a logical relation that is
  \begin{enumerate*}[(i)]
    \item Compositional (\ie validates all the type system rules);
    \item Semantic (\ie expresses the meaning of a judgment entirely as constraints on the behavior of untyped processes); and
    \item Liberal (\ie accepts arbitrary processes as long as they are well-behaved on their external interface).
  \end{enumerate*}
  We establish adequacy of the relation for deadlock freedom and
  exercise it on illustrative examples.
  To evaluate the approach, we extend the core calculus with branching, recursion, unrestricted channels and linking.
  Most of our core results are mechanized in Agda.
\end{abstract}
