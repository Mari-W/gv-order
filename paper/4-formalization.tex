
\section{Mechanization}
\label{sec:mechanization}

Everything this paper states is mechanized in Rocq: the calculus of
\cref{sec:calculus}, the logical relation of
\cref{sec:logicalRelation}, the extensions of \cref{sec:extensions},
and every definition, lemma, theorem and example we display along the
way.
The development is built on top of the \texttt{std++} library and is
free of axioms.

We used generative AI (Claude Code, Fabel 5 and Opus 4.8/5) to produce the proofs. We double-checked all definitions to make sure they match their hand written counter parts.

The artifact comprises roughly $17{,}000$ lines (non-blank, non-comment)
of Rocq across 28 files, containing about $300$ definitions and $790$
lemmas and theorems.
\Cref{tab:mechanization} gives the breakdown by component.

Two entries dominate, and both are informative about where the
difficulty of this style of result lies.

The first is \ruleLabel{connect} (\cref{lem:comp:connect}), which
alone accounts for $2{,}041$ lines of the $3{,}003$ for compositionality of processes.
Connecting two components over a channel is the linear-logic cut, and
it is the only place where the two sides of a composition can
constrain each other.

The second is the examples, at $41\%$ of the development.
This is not incidental: a process is admitted by the relation only if
every transition it can take discharges its obligation, so an
example is a proof about a whole reachable state space.
The load balancer of \cref{ex:load-balancer} takes $2{,}270$ lines and
the leader election of \cref{sec:extension:-sums} $1{,}981$, both
dominated by the case analysis over interleavings.
